## 从上古汉语介词的疑问代词宾语移动现象看介词“于”的性质

**Chen, Weijia** & Anqi Zhang. 2026. “The Nature of *Yu* in Archaic Chinese: Evidence from *Wh*-Movement in Prepositional Phrases.” The 32nd Annual Conference of the International Association of Chinese Linguistics (IACL-32), Guangdong University of Foreign Studies, Guangzhou, China.

<details class="talk-abstract" markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract</summary>

*Wh*-objects in *yu* PPs require pied-piping of the entire PP to the edge of *v*P. Comparisons with *yi* and *zi* suggest that Archaic Chinese PPs were not uniform in their internal structure.

</details>

## Emergence of Evaluative Completive *Hao* in Mandarin

Zhang, Anqi & **Weijia Chen**. 2025. *Expressivity: Variation and Change*, 47th Annual Meeting of the German Linguistic Society (DGfS), Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz, Mainz, Germany.

<details class="talk-abstract" markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract</summary>

Completive markers can contribute speaker-oriented attitudes in several East Asian languages. Drawing on diachronic evidence, this paper traces the development of V-*hao* in Chinese and argues that *hao* developed from a subjective evaluative adjective into an aspectual completive. The change illustrates one possible pathway in the development of multidimensional meaning among completive markers.

</details>

## *Wh*-movements Concerning Prepositional Phrases in Archaic Chinese

**Chen, Weijia.** 2023. 8th Asian Junior Linguists Conference, National University of Singapore, Singapore.

<details class="talk-abstract" markdown="1">
<summary>Abstract</summary>

*Wh*-object preposing in Archaic Chinese does not apply uniformly inside prepositional phrases. Focusing on *yu* and *yi*, this paper proposes that PPs containing *wh*-objects may move as a whole to a focus position at the edge of *v*P and relates their different word orders to differences in grammaticalization and syntactic status.

</details>
